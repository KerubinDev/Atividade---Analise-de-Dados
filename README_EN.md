# Data Analysis: Plant Growth with Different Water Types

This project investigates how different types of water affect bean growth, using simple experiments and basic data analysis techniques.

## Experiment Overview

In this experiment, we studied the growth of bean plants when watered with three different types of water:
- Tap water
- Mineral water
- Sugar water (1 teaspoon of sugar in 250ml of water)

The experiment lasted for 14 days, with daily measurements of plant height, leaf count, and qualitative observations about plant appearance.

## Materials Used

- 30 bean seeds
- 6 transparent plastic cups
- Cotton
- 3 types of water (tap, mineral, sugar solution)
- Millimeter ruler
- Smartphone with camera
- Notebook for data recording

## Methodology

1. We prepared 6 plastic cups with moistened cotton
2. We placed 5 bean seeds in each cup
3. We divided the cups into 3 groups of 2 cups:
   - Group A: watered with tap water
   - Group B: watered with mineral water
   - Group C: watered with sugar water
4. All cups were placed in a location with indirect natural light
5. We watered the cups with their respective water types every 2 days
6. We measured plant height daily for 14 days
7. We photographed the cups every 2 days

## Data Collection

For each group of plants, we recorded:
1. Height of each plant in millimeters
2. Number of leaves
3. Leaf color (light green, medium green, dark green)
4. Germination rate (how many seeds germinated out of 10)

## Data Analysis

Using Excel/Google Sheets, we:
1. Calculated mean and standard deviation of plant height in each group
2. Created charts showing average growth over time
3. Compared germination rates between groups
4. Analyzed differences in leaf count between groups

## Key Findings

Our analysis revealed that:
1. Plants watered with mineral water (Group B) showed the highest germination rate
2. Plants watered with tap water (Group A) grew taller on average
3. Plants watered with sugar water (Group C) developed fewer leaves and showed signs of stress

These findings suggest that the mineral content in water plays an important role in plant development, while excessive sugar can inhibit healthy growth.

## Conclusions

This project demonstrates how a simple experiment can generate interesting data for analysis. By systematically measuring plant growth and analyzing the results, we can draw conclusions about the effect of different water types on plant development.

Data analysis does not need to be complex to be valuable - even basic tools like spreadsheets can reveal important patterns in our experiments.
